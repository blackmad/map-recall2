# Quick photo → building recipe contract

Use this checklist for each batch authoring pass. Aim for recognizable structure in one quick pass; reuse library assemblies. Do not spend time on tiny offsets before the facade is recognizable.

1. Inspect the dated full crop, roof crop and ground crop together. Use wider context when width, neighbors, roof or entrance are cut off. Compare selected frontage width with the owner footprint. A photo covering only part of an owner requires separate frontage parts or an extraction repair; do not stretch it across the owner.
2. Count ground level, upper body rows and attic lights separately. Count bays per row. Keep shorter top windows and independent floor schedules. Ground is not another repeated upper row.
3. Select the street silhouette: level cornice, mansard, triangular, neck, bell, stepped or shallow pediment. Owner maximum height can belong to a rear roof. Avoid a full-width blank attic block. Explicitly infer the roof behind the visible front; retain raw BAG evidence and placement.
4. Select an entrance/display/garage pattern from the library. Residential buildings normally need an entrance. Record a side-entry exception if evidence supports it. Match left/right arrangement, number of doors and raised thresholds before ornament.
5. Choose wall brick/render, ground wall/columns, window frame, sash, door panels and glass separately. Continue upper brick at ground unless a distinct wall finish is visible. Dark glazing or doors do not imply a dark wall band; white window frames do not imply a white wall band.
6. Choose actual joinery: single sash, paired sash, broad three-pane group, warehouse loading arch and shutters, or industrial grid. Use independent warehouse/modern intervals for a mixed frontage. Add visible cornices, bands and balconies from shared components.
7. Use centred/even spacing when evidence is ambiguous and no offset is visible. Never move the source frontage to hide an unexplained residual owner strip. Flag coverage/stepped-plane uncertainty instead.
8. Record photo date, source hash and inferred dimensions. Build, inspect the front and a useful quarter view, and refresh the progress page. Check the generated GLB corresponds to the recipe. A layout edit remains fidelity pending until visually reviewed; artifact generation alone is not acceptance.

Before advancing a batch, consult `recipe-review-warnings.json`. Resolve or explain native roof volume, blank upper facade, entrance and distinct ground finish warnings. These warnings prioritize review; they cannot determine photo likeness by themselves.

Check capture registration before counting bays: a clipped neighboring window does not justify deleting an own bay. Reproject a wider full-height view from a different cached capture when possible. Scaffold poles, stitching seams, flags and projecting objects are not window/wall evidence. Check any apparent centre feature in another dated capture before choosing a wide-centre pattern. Do not assign green masonry from vines or foliage: sample exposed brick/render and keep plants separate. An authored gable with a retained native roof requires a front/quarter overlap check; triangles crossing its steps are generator/ownership failures, never architectural decoration. Trees or partial owner coverage require an explicit unresolved schedule, not a claim that stock residential rows were observed.

Ground piers use the shared `pilaster` shaft, base and capital; pale piers between dark doors remain pale geometry independently of the wall and door leaf colours. A projecting entrance surround does not yet establish a recessed passage: keep that separate limitation visible.

Converted warehouses can retain a central loading/balcony rhythm with very small dark flank lights. `warehouse-stack` accepts independent `loadingTemplate`, `flankTemplate` and `crestTemplate`: use these for rectangular centre glazing, separate pane divisions and restrained side frames. Keep body and attic patterns separate where aperture heights differ. A balcony stack and pale heads can share that centre line; count all visible body levels in a full-height alternate capture rather than copying the starter's three-row schedule. Physical balcony recess depth and masonry accents still need their own assembly choices.

For a pale outlined curved gable, select `frontages[].coping` (width/depth/colour/provenance) rather than substituting a triangular roof. The portable and Blender consumers share the same continuous band. The quick editor exposes pale/dark/none/preserve. For independent floor/window groups or frontage parts, edit the recipe directly; the uniform controls intentionally remain unavailable.

Use `flat-with-dormers` for a level street cornice with raised projecting roof lights. Keep the body frontage at eaves; `details.appearanceDormers` owns the raised glazing, cheeks and cap. Select central or paired in the quick editor, then check the roof footprint against the owner. Depth and hidden flat support remain inferred. For a two-level upper box, select the `roof-box` attachment assembly with explicit front window rows; do not squeeze it into a single dormer or add a blank full-width storey. Keep distinct dormer wall/frame/roof colours when the photo supports them.

Roof boxes use `details.appearanceDormers[].assembly="roof-box"`, explicit `frontWindows` fractions within the box, and distinct wall/frame/glass finishes. `baseZ` and bounded `frontProjection` allow a photo-observed projecting base below eaves while the inboard footprint retains explicit roof support. Check the front and quarter view: a projecting pale box must not become a full-width brick attic. The quick editor preserves an existing offset and window schedule when changing height. Hidden depth and support remain inferred.

For unequal adjacent house fronts, author `massing.appearanceRoof.parts[]` with fractions and separate eaves/peak heights. Do not give the tall front a covering based on the short front's eaves. Keep each part inside the explicit owner height envelope. The shared generator closes the height step; hidden pitches still require an inferred provenance note. Uniform height controls must not flatten an independent roof schedule.

Read entrance leaves separately from surrounding walls and frames. A dark paneled residential door beneath a glazed transom uses `doorLeaf` with a photo-selected timber colour; it is not a full-height glass pane. Ground patterns support separate `doorTemplate` and `windowTemplate`, so entry leaf settings never spread to displays. `four-doors` is available for a pale four-leaf frontage such as Bloemstraat154. Preserve surrounding brick or pale facade finish independently. Plain/panelled leaves remain simplified; side entrances, garage/warehouse closures and glazed shop doors need their own evidence-led choice.

A small pitched cap above a rectangular roof light uses `capStyle="pitched"` and bounded `capHeight`; this differs from legacy `capRise`, which curves the aperture/head. Set the attachment body height so the closed pitched cap reaches the appearance peak. The shared cap vertices feed Blender and portable export. Roof boxes currently keep flat caps. Uniform quick height edits reject pitched caps to preserve their schedule; edit Recipe JSON or explicitly choose a replacement dormer. Hoists supported by a roof light need a separate attachment support binding; do not attach them to a lower frontage whose head cannot support them.

Bind a roof-light hoist with `supportAttachmentId` and `supportRegion="body"` or `"cap"`. Body anchors must fit solid wall outside its windows; cap support requires a pitched cap and bounds both beam width and anchor heights inside its triangular section. Rear embedment must fit depth and avoid glazing. This moves the beam/bracket to the attachment front plane in both consumers. Primary frontage frame only; inferred dimensions remain explicit. Edit the attachment and hoist together in Recipe JSON, since a uniform roof-height control cannot safely move a bound beam.

When upper rows become implausibly tall, do not distribute the full BAG owner maximum over the visible window rows. Identify ground head, upper row spacing, street cornice and separate roof light first; author an explicitly inferred street envelope when the dated photo conflicts with the native envelope. The review flags upper storeys above 4.2m for inspection, rather than automatically lowering real tall rooms. Count bays only between the target facade's boundaries in a wider capture: a clipped neighbour must not become a third bay. Ground opening presets never establish wall material. Set continuous exposed brick explicitly when visible, and assign pale frames, columns and dark door leaves to their own components. Four dark timber leaves must not become two entrances plus two generic display windows.

For asymmetric rectangular windows, use an off-centre mullion and explicit sashBars segments [x1,z1,x2,z2] in normalized aperture fractions when a horizontal division exists only in the narrow side pane. Fractions in z run upward from the bottom. Set transom=null when no full-width division exists. Mirror the side-pane split per opening when the photo shows paired inward-facing lights. The shared renderer supports at most24 nonzero axis-aligned segments inside rectangular window apertures; curved openings, doors and warehouse leaves require their own assemblies. Neither the window colour nor a plant on a parapet establishes surrounding masonry colour.

Persistent projecting bays need their own three-dimensional assembly. Recheck an alternate wider capture when image stitching or occlusion makes the feature ambiguous; persistence in another capture supports architecture, without proving metric depth. Use groupedBays with existing openingIds for every front row; the bay owns those openings and the flat wall must not receive duplicate joinery. Explicit construction="hollow" shares pierced front/angled-return/rear panels, opaque recessed panes and closed base/roof between Blender and portable export. This construction requires a level base and shellThickness .16-.29m bounded by projection. Panels are individually closed solids with overlapping joints; no single Boolean-unioned manifold claim is made. Legacy solid/tapered construction remains separate. A pale projecting bay does not make surrounding ground brick pale. Count visible body rows independently from upper roof strips and bay head/spandrel panels.

Owner projected width is a coverage warning, not a count of missing full-height bays. Distinguish low offset source extensions from upper wall planes by their native vertical bounds. Nearly parallel but angled street walls can require independent facade frames; a fixed depth-span tolerance misses them as width grows. The coverage diagnostic uses a bounded15-degree direction check and nearby-wall limits, reports wall scope/height/angle, and never automatically stretches the selected frontage. Compare original and alternate captures before authoring: if cornice/roof, row counts and neighbouring facade forms conflict, mark the alternate identityReview.status="rejected" with a concrete reason. Retain it as diagnostic evidence; never use it as the primary authoring photo or silently transfer its openings/colours to the current owner.


Changing an opening assembly must preserve the surrounding wall finish. Warehouse loading leaves, residential doors, glazing and frame colours are independent of masonry. Assign a contrasting ground-wall finish only from exposed wall evidence; foundation defaults continue the upper wall. On delivery, compare the current recipe/GLB and published embedded preview hashes so an older screenshot is distinguished from an unapplied correction.


For adjacent level fronts of different heights, use appearanceRoof.kind="flat-stepped" with positive parts[].fraction summing to one and an explicit eaves height per part. Set owner eaves to the minimum and peak to the maximum part height. The authored frontage profile must follow each horizontal part with a vertical junction. Do not add a fictitious pitch or spread a taller neighbour's floor schedule across the shorter front. Use independent opening-row storeys and scoped centres.

A rectangular materialZones[] finish with construction="pierced" uses shared closed aperture geometry in Blender and portable export. Define exposed wall colour independently of nearby glazing, shutters and vegetation. Every intersecting opening must lie wholly inside the zone; a boundary crossing an aperture is rejected rather than covering glass. Legacy Boolean zones retain their existing Blender construction. Reuse pierced zones for mixed render/brick owner fronts, keeping source shell and placement immutable.


A visibly deep entrance is a passage assembly rather than a large black glazed door. Use a rectangular ground opening with entranceRecess: explicit depth, liningThickness/liningColour, doorWidthFraction/doorHeightFraction, thresholdRise and a restricted doorTemplate. The outer aperture cuts the street wall; solid returns, floor and soffit lead to a pierced rear wall and smaller door. Door-leaf colour belongs to the rear joinery, independently of masonry lining. All dimensions remain inferred until registration. Do not combine an outer doorLeaf, warehouse assembly or curved head with this recess. Inspect quarter view and streetward sightlines before accepting it.


For raised entrances, place the door threshold independently of the basement and keep its height plausible relative to nearby upper windows. entranceSteps[] now shares solid or open stairs, optional supported landing and rail between Blender and portable export. Use direction outward/from-left/from-right from the dated photo. Rise must meet openingId's threshold. An open stair leaves the basement below visible through discrete treads/stringers instead of sealing it with stacked blocks. Crossing the selected frontage interval requires scope="same-owner-cross-frontage", explicit inferred provenance and an owner-width bound; do not bind stairs to an unrelated neighbouring entrance. Run, structure and railing dimensions remain inferred unless registered.

Arched residential entrances: retain the segmental/rounded head when visible, using `doorLeaf` for the opaque timber below a glazed transom. Set `transom` so height × transom is at least the arch rise; the leaf must end below the spring. Never flatten an observed arch just because the opaque leaf is rectangular. Distinguish body window rows from a narrow attic front above a level cornice; the attic is not a full-width extra storey. Anjeliersstraat 149 is a dated-photo example (two body rows, three bays, right entrance, narrow stepped attic). All physical proportions remain inferred until registered.

Partial ground finishes: use `materialZones[].construction: "clipped"` for a thin colour/material band crossing apertures. Bind its colour and height to visible masonry evidence; vegetation, door leaves, glass and curtains are separate. The shared builder subtracts original aperture triangles from the finish rectangle, preserving every intersecting door/window void and closing the resulting notched boundary. Do not use a full-height storefront colour to represent lower stone/render returns. `pierced` remains for wholly contained openings; `clipped` supports open boundary cuts.


Lighter rendering levels must preserve the authored architecture and evidence scope. Joinery construction carries explicit LOD roles shared by Blender and portable capture; do not choose components by names, recolour them, merge doors with glazing or remove aperture silhouettes. Facade retains sash divisions and opacity; massing can omit sash divisions while retaining outer trim/glazing and opaque leaves. Unknown/source geometry and owner extent-defining components stay. Cornices, roof boxes, gable forms, entrances, stairs and finishes retain their source/inferred provenance. LOD checks do not clear photographic registration or likeness holds.
