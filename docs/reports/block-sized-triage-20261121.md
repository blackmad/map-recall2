# Block-sized 1985+ housing: kit or hand (2026-11-21)

Contact sheets: `artifacts/block-sized-triage/sheet-01.jpg` to `sheet-04.jpg` (16 tiles each, payoff order); overview `docs/reports/block-sized-triage-20261121.jpg`. Machine-readable rows: `docs/reports/block-sized-triage-20261121.json`.

## Method

- **Selection.** Rows of `core` and `outer` in `docs/reports/ordinary-backlog-20261120.json` with `use == "housing"`, `year >= 1985` and either `streetWallM >= 150` or `footprintM2 >= 3000`. That gives 64 buildings: 23 from 1985-2004 and 41 from 2005 on.
- **Photos.** Gemeente Amsterdam street panoramas (CC BY 4.0), rectified to a straight-on wall crop by `scripts/pand-reference/run.ts` (the shared `.cache/pand-reference` cache). run.ts often picks a short end wall or an old capture. When that happened, `scripts/pand-reference/all-walls.ts` cropped the three longest exposed walls (`artifacts/block-sized-triage/walls/`), and I judged the longest road-facing wall with a usable view. Three buildings had only pre-completion captures (a building site or scaffolding) until I reran with `--date=2023,2024,2025` (`walls2/`). For 29 buildings the crop came from 2024-2025; the rest range from 2016 to 2023. The "photo" column gives the capture year and source for each row. "Oblique" means the camera was more than 20 degrees off the wall normal.
- **Counting.** A front is a stretch of the main wall that differs from its neighbours in material or colour, in window system, or in both. A repeated identical module counts once. A plinth, or an attic set back in the same system, does not count as a separate front unless it changes material and window system too.
- **Route.** "kit" means fewer than 3 distinct fronts on one wall plane. "hand" means 3 or more (proud panels or loggias only matter when 3 or more fronts are present). "kit (unverified)" means the crop is mostly blocked by trees. Confidence is high, med or low; trees, wash-out, obliquity and short walls lower it.

## What the kit would still need

These features recur across the hand-routed blocks, and many also recur on kit-routed ones:

1. **Several fronts on one wall.** The main hand driver: several architect fronts share one BAG wall (Westerdok, Rapenburgerstraat, Sumatrakade, Rinus Michelslaan, Tweede Atjehstraat, Revaleiland). The kit needs a wall split into measured segments, each with its own kit front (material, window system, height, roofline step), instead of one front per wall.
2. **Projecting glazed bay stacks (oriels).** Full-height glazed bays, often white- or red-framed, standing proud of brick (Rapenburgerstraat 4-94, Valkenburgerstraat, Pieter de Hoochstraat, Oostelijke Handelskade/Klapmutsenveem, Klaprozenweg's bronze window boxes).
3. **Proud render or cladding panels.** Coloured planes (mauve, grey, white) standing proud of the brick and framing large glazing (Rapenburgerstraat 220-326 and 4-94).
4. **Deep staggered cantilever slab balconies.** Including full-length wrap-around slabs and stepped terraces (Rinus Michelslaan 1-343, Nida Senffstraat, Spadinalaan, Gustav Mahlerlaan, Westerdok's timber-soffit balconies and stepped terrace stack). This is the dominant read on several kit-routed blocks too.
5. **Glass-box loggias and coloured spandrel bands.** Enclosed glazed loggia boxes (Hoofdweg 458-576), rainbow spandrel bands in a dark brick frame (Sumatrakade).
6. **Warehouse pastiche vocabulary.** Tiers of round-arched windows with stone imposts, cornices and pilaster grids, arched ground floors, plus large brick arches over passages (Oostelijke Handelskade, Van der Palmkade, Krommeniestraat).
7. **Neoclassical roof and ground pieces.** Mansards with pedimented dormers, stone colonnade ground floors, cast-iron curved balconies (Cornelis Vermuydenstraat, Piri Reisplein, Veemkade 366-556 colonnade, Bos en Lommerplein pilotis).
8. **Row of varied house fronts.** One pand reading as 8 or more individually designed canal-house-style fronts (Wiborgeiland). A row recipe that draws kit houses side by side would cover it better than hand modelling.
9. **Odd glazing systems.** A glass-fin curtain wall (Funenpark), a greenhouse glass attic (Pieter de Hoochstraat), wavy curved massing in banded brick (Spadinalaan 483-699).

## Status after lane large-kit-gaps-20261121 (commit b4f3914d07)

- **1, several fronts on one wall:** partly closed. `walls[].houses[]` now carry their own window preset, ribbon,
  blank and brick storeys, bay windows, ground and wall material inside one 3DBAG panel. A roofline step between
  fronts still needs a separate 3DBAG panel (houses share the panel's eaves).
- **3, proud render or cladding panels:** closed (`renderPanels`, 0.2 m proud by default, own colour, round the windows).
- **5, coloured spandrel bands:** closed as `accentPanels` (flush per-cell panels). Glass-box loggias are still open;
  loggias can now be lined in their own colour with a door-and-window back wall.
- Not closed: 2 (oriels), 4 (deep staggered cantilever slabs), 6-9.
- The route rule is unchanged (fewer than 3 fronts = kit). On Valkenburgerstraat, the A2 kit recipe beat the old kit
  in a blind A/B (4 of 5 walls, 1 tie), but hand model B is still the one installed. The 13 hand blocks are worth a
  second look as kit-plus-houses candidates once a row of them has been compared blind against a hand model.
