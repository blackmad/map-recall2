De Pijp

De Pijp (English: The Pipe) is a neighbourhood of Amsterdam, Netherlands. It is located directly south of Amsterdam's city centre and it is part of the borough Amsterdam-Zuid, in a part of the city known as the Old South (Oud Zuid). It is served by De Pijp metro station. Most streets in De Pijp are named after Dutch painters, like Jan Steen, Frans Hals, Ruysdael and Vincent van Gogh. en.wikipedia.org

The name. De Pijp means "The Pipe" in English and the origin of the name is not well documented, but it is likely a reference to the long, narrow streets that funneled people towards central Amsterdam. en.wikipedia.org

The stretch between the Amsteldijk and the Sweelinckstraat was formerly called Hoedemakersstraat (council decision of Nieuwer-Amstel, 20 March 1883) and was numbered from the Amsteldijk, as the Kuipersstraat and other nearby streets laid out by Nieuwer-Amstel before 1896 still are. When this stretch was renamed Van Ostadestraat in 1904, it had to be renumbered.
  • Van WoustraatGeert van Wou (c. 1450-1527), renowned bell-founder from Kampen.
  • VerbindingstraatThe names express the function. The dam links the IJ island, laid out in 1898, with the Handelskade. The street was laid out before the great annexation of 1896, so that residents of Nieuwer-Amstel living here on the edge of Amsterdam could reach their town hall on the Amsteldijk over their own municipality's ground.
  • Vincent van GoghstraatVincent Willem van Gogh (1853-1890), painter and draughtsman, who worked first in the Borinage and Drenthe before leaving for France in 1886, where he made most of his best-known paintings. He was of great importance for the development of painting.
  • WesteindeNamed after its position: west of the Utrechtsepoort.
  • WeteringlaanThe streets lie in the Weteringbuurt. The names recall the Wetering, which originally flowed from outside into the Spui; the Boerenwetering is a remnant of it. The Schans was once the rampart on the inside of the city wall. In the first half of the nineteenth century the walls and ramparts were demolished. Parts were turned into promenades (Eerste and Tweede Weteringplantsoen), and a road was built that was at first called the Schans along its whole length but later renamed Marnixstraat, Weteringschans and Sarphatistraat. The Nieuwe Weteringstraat was laid out on the site freed by the demolition of the flour and bread factory.
  • Willem PasstoorsstraatWillem C.J. Passtoors (1856-1916), Amsterdam tobacconist, who in 1888 founded the Roman Catholic People's League in the Netherlands and so became the founder of the Roman Catholic labour movement in the country. From 1901 to 1913 he was a member of the House of Representatives.
